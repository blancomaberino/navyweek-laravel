# Comcast — Military & Veteran Discount & Maximum-Savings Brief

**Researched:** July 8, 2026 · **Baseline for all math:** one military household's connectivity + entertainment spend — Xfinity Internet + 1 mobile line + one live-TV/streaming service — measured as effective 12-month value, since telecom "savings" are recurring, not a single cart.

> **Page role:** This page answers the searcher who types **"Comcast military discount"** (the parent company). Comcast's consumer offers are delivered under its **Xfinity** brand, so the actual discount detail lives on NavyWeek's dedicated **[Xfinity Military & Veteran Discount](/xfinity-military-discount/)** page. This brief frames the Comcast → Xfinity relationship, adds the **parent-company** context Xfinity's page doesn't cover (Internet Essentials, Peacock, Universal Destinations, Comcast NBCUniversal's hiring/giving), and routes the reader to the right page rather than duplicating it.

## Headline finding

**There is no separate "Comcast" military discount — Comcast's consumer brand is Xfinity, and that is where the military offer lives.** Comcast Corporation's own military page confirms the offer is an Xfinity **perks bundle**, not a percentage or dollar discount: verified service members and military families with **Xfinity Internet** get a **free Xfinity Mobile line (24 months)**, **NOW TV free for 1 year** (125+ channels), an automatic upgrade to **Xfinity Diamond Rewards** (which includes **Peacock Premium at no extra cost**), and access to **Universal Destinations** military pricing. Verification happens by signing in at **xfinity.com/military**. Beyond the Xfinity offer, Comcast the parent also runs **Internet Essentials** low-cost broadband (as low as **$14.95/mo**) that many military families qualify for, and a standalone **Peacock military discount ($3.99/mo for a year)** for those not on Diamond. Archetype: this is a **parent-brand routing page**; the underlying offer is a **membership perks bundle layered on paid service** (not a clean discount %), with a **needs-based low-cost-broadband** secondary path. The single most important caveat carries over from the Xfinity page: **the perks require an Xfinity Internet subscription** — it is a benefits package on paid service, not a standalone coupon.

## Stacking Rules (verified)
1. **The perks bundle is layered onto paid Xfinity Internet, not stacked with a coupon.** There is no percentage/dollar military discount to combine with anything. (xfinity.com/learn/deals/military; corporate.comcast.com)
2. **Internet Essentials is a separate, needs-based program**, not a military discount and not combinable with the Xfinity military perks on the same account — they are different service tiers. Treat as an either/or connectivity choice. (corporate.comcast.com)
3. **Peacock at $3.99/mo is an alternative to the Diamond-included Peacock, not an addition** — Comcast frames the $3.99 rate as for those "not enrolled in Diamond Rewards." (corporate.comcast.com)
4. **Universal Destinations discounts are a distinct NBCUniversal program** with their own verification and terms; they do not stack with the Xfinity service offer. (corporate.comcast.com)
5. **No retail-style stacks exist** (no cashback portal, no GovX, no promo-code stacking) — this is service, not merchandise.

### FAQ

#### Does Comcast offer a military discount?
Comcast delivers its consumer offers under the **Xfinity** brand, so the "Comcast military discount" is the **Xfinity** offer — a perks bundle (free Xfinity Mobile line for 24 months, NOW TV free for a year, and Diamond Rewards with Peacock Premium) for verified service members and families with Xfinity Internet. See our full **[Xfinity Military Discount](/xfinity-military-discount/)** page for details.

#### Is Comcast the same as Xfinity?
Xfinity is Comcast's consumer brand for Internet, Mobile, and TV. When you see "Comcast military discount," it refers to Xfinity's offer. Comcast Corporation's own military page directs customers to xfinity.com/military.

#### How much is the Comcast/Xfinity military discount?
There's no flat percentage or dollar amount — the value is in the included services: a free Mobile line for 24 months, a free year of NOW TV, and Diamond Rewards including Peacock Premium (a stated $60/yr value). A $180 Visa gift card from the 2024 launch is not confirmed on the current official page.

#### Do veterans, spouses, and dependents qualify?
Yes. Xfinity lists veterans, active/reserve members, retirees, reservists, Gold Star families, and spouse/family members in the household of someone who qualifies.

#### How do I verify my military status?
Sign in at **xfinity.com/military** with your Xfinity ID and confirm your status. Verification is processed through SheerID (per Xfinity's support docs); status questions go to 1-833-231-6446.

#### Does Comcast use ID.me, GovX, SheerID, or WeSalute?
Per Xfinity's support documentation, verification runs through **SheerID**. (Comcast/NBCUniversal also appears on the WeSalute marketplace, but the official offer routes verification through the xfinity.com/military sign-in.)

#### Does Comcast have a discount for low-income military families?
Yes — separately from the military offer, Comcast's **Internet Essentials** program provides broadband **as low as $14.95/month** and, per Comcast, connects military families to the internet. It's needs-based (income/assistance eligibility), not a military benefit, but many military families qualify.

#### What about Peacock or Universal theme parks?
Comcast owns NBCUniversal, so its offers reach beyond Internet. Eligible military customers not on Diamond Rewards can get **Peacock for $3.99/month for a year**, and active service members, retirees, and spouses can get **discounted Universal Destinations** park tickets and vacation packages (verify through the park's own military channel).

#### What's actually the cheapest way for a service member to save with Comcast?
If you already have (or want) Xfinity Internet and can verify, take the perks bundle. If cost is the constraint, check **Internet Essentials ($14.95/mo)** first. If you only want Peacock, use the **$3.99/mo** military rate. If you'd rather have a per-line bill discount, compare Verizon/AT&T/T-Mobile.

#### Does Comcast hire veterans?
Yes — as corporate context: Comcast NBCUniversal reports hiring **21,000+** veterans, military spouses, and Guard/Reserve members since 2015 and donating **$200M+** to military organizations since 2011 (including $1M to military nonprofits in 2024). This isn't a customer discount, but it speaks to the company's military ties.
