## Headline finding

**No — the Venture X is not free for military members who open it while on active duty.** Capital One has no MLA annual-fee waiver: an active-duty servicemember who applies today pays the full **$395/year**, while the same person could hold an Amex Platinum ($895 list) or Chase Sapphire Reserve ($795 list) at **$0** under those issuers' MLA policies. That is lawful — 32 CFR 232.4(d) lets issuers exclude bona fide, reasonable annual fees from the MLA's 36% MAPR, so Amex and Chase waive voluntarily and Capital One chooses not to. The one real fee-relief path is **SCRA**: on accounts opened **before** your active-duty start date, Capital One goes beyond the statute — **4% interest cap** (law requires 6%) and **no fees except bona-fide insurance** (annual fee effectively $0), retroactive to your active-duty start ("order to" date for reservists), lasting until **one year after** active duty ends, by request (online or 1-855-CAP1-MIL). Guard/Reserve members can exploit the timing: a card opened **between activations** is pre-service for the next 30+ day activation.

The honest consolation math: even unwaived, the Venture X roughly pays for itself **if you actually use its credits** — the $300 Capital One Travel credit plus 10,000 anniversary miles (≈$100 toward travel) ≈ $400/year against the $395 fee. That's a conditional break-even, not a discount, and the page must say so plainly.

Archetype: **regulated-benefit / statutory-relief brand** (same family as the American Express, Amex Platinum, Chase, and Capital One issuer pages) — with a twist: relief exists on the **SCRA side only**, and the secondary story is the **"worth the fee anyway?" premium-card math** for readers who can't get the waiver.

### Who Qualifies (for fee relief)
- **Servicemembers whose Venture X predates their active-duty start date** — eligible via SCRA; request required.
- **Guard/Reserve on 30+ day active orders** — eligible on cards opened **before** the activation's "order to" date; each activation is a fresh test.

### How To Get The Fee Waived (SCRA — pre-service accounts only)

### FAQ

#### Is the Capital One Venture X free for military?
Not if you open it while on active duty — Capital One has **no MLA annual-fee waiver**, so the $395 fee stands. It's only effectively free under **SCRA**, and only if the account was opened **before** your active-duty start date (relief runs until one year after active duty ends).

#### Does Capital One waive the Venture X annual fee for active duty?
No. Unlike American Express and Chase, Capital One does not zero annual fees on cards opened during service. Its published SCRA decline criteria reject any account "opened after the active duty start date," and no MLA fee program exists.

#### Why is that legal under the Military Lending Act?
The MLA caps military APR at 36% MAPR, but its rules (32 CFR 232.4(d)) exclude "bona fide," reasonable credit-card fees — including annual fees — from that calculation. Amex and Chase waive fees voluntarily; Capital One doesn't.

#### How do I get the Venture X fee waived under SCRA?
The account must predate your active-duty start date. Request online at capitalone.com/military, by phone at **1-855-CAP1-MIL (1-855-227-1645)** (24/7), by mail, or in a branch, with your orders or a commanding-officer letter. Decision within 2 monthly billing cycles; relief is retroactive to your active-duty start ("order to") date and lasts until a year after active duty ends.

#### I'm in the Guard or Reserve — can I still get it free?
Yes, with timing: open the card **before** a 30+ day activation, then request SCRA once on orders. Coverage keys to your "order to" date, and each new activation re-tests only whether the account predates *that* activation — so a card opened between activations qualifies for the next one.

#### Can I upgrade an old Capital One card to Venture X and keep my SCRA waiver?
Cardholders report yes — a product change keeps the same account and open date, so SCRA reportedly carries over (usually forfeiting the welcome offer). Capital One doesn't document this; confirm at 1-855-227-1645 before counting on it.

#### Is the Venture X worth $395 if my fee isn't waived?
Roughly break-even **if you use the credits**: the $300 annual Capital One Travel credit plus 10,000 anniversary miles (≈$100 toward travel) ≈ $400/year against the $395 fee, and year one adds the welcome offer (75,000 miles as of July 19, 2026). If you wouldn't book through Capital One Travel, the fee is just money out the door.

#### Should I get the Venture X or the Amex Platinum / Chase Sapphire Reserve while on active duty?
For fee value, Amex and Chase win outright: the Amex Platinum ($895 list) is $0 automatically under MLA, and Chase's premium cards (Sapphire Reserve, $795 list) are letter-confirmed at $0 for covered borrowers — while a during-service Venture X pays $395. See our Amex Platinum and Chase guides.

#### Do military spouses get the Venture X fee waived?
Not on their own separately opened cards — SCRA covers the member's pre-service obligations and **joint** member-spouse obligations only, and a January 2026 community report documents Capital One declining a spouse's own card. Spouses can submit the request on the member's behalf. (Contrast: under Amex/Chase MLA policies, spouses qualify on their own cards.)

#### Does Capital One use ID.me, SheerID, or GovX for this?
No. There's no verification service and no promo code — SCRA relief is requested with your active-duty orders or a CO letter, reviewed by Capital One directly.

#### What do I still get if I'm paying the fee?
Everything the card advertises: $300/yr Capital One Travel credit, 10,000 anniversary miles, up to $120 Global Entry/TSA PreCheck credit every 4 years, Capital One Lounge + Priority Pass access, and 2X miles everywhere (10X hotels/rental cars, 5X flights via Capital One Travel). Since February 1, 2026, additional-cardholder lounge access costs $125/yr each.
