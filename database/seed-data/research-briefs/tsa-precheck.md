# TSA PreCheck — Military Discount & Maximum-Savings Brief

**Researched:** July 14, 2026 · **Baseline cart for all math:** one **5-year TSA PreCheck membership** at the standard new-enrollment fee (**$76.75–$85.00** depending on provider, per tsa.gov, July 14, 2026).

## Headline finding

TSA PreCheck is a government program with **no "veteran discount" — but four distinct military paths, three of them free.** (1) **Uniformed service members and DoD civilians fly PreCheck free** with no enrollment at all — the DoD ID number goes in the Known Traveler Number field. (2) **Military spouses get $25 off** enrollment or renewal at all three providers. (3) **Military survivor (Gold Star) families and qualifying disabled veterans enroll free** — the latter under the VETS Safe Travel Act (Public Law 118-238, signed January 4, 2025), IDEMIA only. (4) **Most veterans get nothing**: the benefit ends when you leave DoD, and the standard fee applies — so the real veteran play is a **credit card that reimburses the fee** (dozens do), which makes PreCheck effectively free anyway. This is a government program, not a brand — no ID.me/GovX, no stacking, no sales.

## Stacking Rules (verified, program terms)
1. **Nothing stacks.** These are federal enrollment fees — one waiver or discount per person, no coupons, no cashback portals track government enrollment sites.
2. **The active-duty benefit and an enrolled membership are separate systems.** Serving members shouldn't buy a membership; conversely, the DoD ID number stops working as a KTN after separation — the two don't bridge.
3. **The VETS Safe waiver is IDEMIA-only.** "TSA will not issue a reimbursement to applicants who enroll or renew with CLEAR or Telos" (tsa.gov). Renewals stay free while eligibility holds.
4. **The spouse discount requires eligibility documentation at enrollment** — USID card showing "Spouse" + Active Duty/Reserve/Guard sponsor affiliation; at some IDEMIA locations it's a pay-then-rebate flow (email receipt + ID, refund within ~5 business days).
5. **Credit-card reimbursement layers with nothing but replaces everything** for the ordinary veteran — the card credits the fee whatever provider you pick.

### Intro
Is there a TSA PreCheck veteran discount? For most veterans, no — but that's the least interesting part of the answer. If you're **currently serving** (active duty, Guard, Reserve, Coast Guard, academy cadets, USPHS or NOAA commissioned corps) or a **DoD civilian**, you already have PreCheck free: your DoD ID number *is* your Known Traveler Number. No enrollment, no fee.

Once you leave the service that benefit ends — TSA says so explicitly — and the standard 5-year enrollment fee applies ($76.75–$85 depending on provider as of July 14, 2026). Everyone else's best move is a credit card that reimburses the fee — TSA keeps the list. ### Who Qualifies
- **Active duty, Reserves, National Guard, Coast Guard + Reserve** — Free, no enrollment; DoD ID as KTN (official and personal travel).
- **Service-academy cadets/midshipmen (USMA, USNA, USAFA, USCGA)** — Free, same mechanism. **USMMA and state maritime academy students** — free via TWIC (CIN as KTN).
- **USPHS and NOAA commissioned officers** — Free, same mechanism.
- **DoD federal civilians and NAF employees** — Free after a one-time opt-in at the ID Card Office Online (milConnect).
- **Veterans / military retirees (general)** — Not free; benefit ends at separation; standard fees apply.
- **Veterans enrolled in VA healthcare with permanent blindness, or who require a VA-issued wheelchair or prosthetic limb** — Free enrollment and renewals (VETS Safe Travel Act), via IDEMIA only.
- **Military spouses (of currently serving members)** — $25 off enrollment or renewal at CLEAR, IDEMIA, or Telos.
- **Military survivor families (Gold Star lapel per 10 USC §1126, Next of Kin Lapel Button, or family of a member who died of service-connected injury/illness after separation)** — Free; each family member needs their own TAPS letter or TSA acknowledgement form.
- **Adult dependents (18+, non-DoD)** — Not free; children 17 and under use the PreCheck lane with an enrolled parent on the same boarding pass.

### How To Redeem — Currently Serving
1. Put your **DoD ID number** (back of your CAC) in the **Known Traveler Number** field: DTS profile, airline frequent-flyer profiles, and every booking.
2. Confirm your reservation name matches your ID exactly.
3. Look for the TSA PreCheck indicator on the boarding pass — you cannot enter the lane by showing your CAC.
4. DoD civilians: opt in first at the ID Card Office Online ("My Profile" → "CIV" tab), one time only.

### How To Redeem — Qualifying Disabled Veteran (free)
1. Sign in to **VA.gov** → Benefit letters and documents → download the **TSA PreCheck Application Fee Waiver Letter** (VA uploads it if you're eligible).
2. Email the letter to **IDEMIA customer support** with "VETS Safe" in the subject line to receive an offer code.
3. Complete the IDEMIA application and in-person visit (fingerprints/photo — TSA accommodates applicants who can't provide ten-print fingerprints). KTN typically arrives in 3–5 days (up to 60).
4. Renewals stay free while you remain eligible — do not enroll via CLEAR or Telos (no reimbursement).

### How To Redeem — Veteran (standard) / Spouse / Survivor Family
1. **Veteran:** check TSA's credit-card list; if a card you hold reimburses the fee, enroll with any provider and pay with that card ($0 net). Otherwise IDEMIA is cheapest ($76.75 new / $58.75 online renewal).
2. **Spouse:** enroll at any provider; bring your USID card showing "Spouse" + Active Duty/Reserve/Guard sponsor; $25 comes off (some IDEMIA sites rebate after payment — email receipt + ID, "Military Spouse" subject).
3. **Survivor family:** get a verification letter from TAPS (800-959-8277 / info@taps.org) or a TSA acknowledgement form (866-289-9673), then follow CLEAR/IDEMIA's offer-code or rebate process.

### How Verification Works
No ID.me, SheerID, or GovX anywhere — verification is by government credential: CAC/DoD ID (serving), milConnect opt-in (civilians), VA-issued letter (disabled veterans), TAPS letter (survivors), USID card (spouses). All enrolled memberships run 5 years; the serving-member benefit needs no renewal — it simply ends at separation.

### Exclusions And Fine Print
- The DoD-ID benefit **does not survive separation or retirement** — enroll like a civilian afterward.
- The VETS Safe waiver is narrow: VA-healthcare-enrolled + permanent blindness or VA-issued wheelchair/prosthetic. Other disability ratings — even 100% P&T — are **not** listed as qualifying (per tsa.gov, July 14, 2026).
- CLEAR's "free TSA PreCheck" requires joining paid CLEAR+ (they refund the $79.95 after both applications) — not free money for most people.
- A discount/waiver never guarantees acceptance — standard TSA vetting applies.
- Fees shown are as of July 14, 2026, and set by federal rule; providers' in-person vs online prices differ.

### Best Ways To Save More
- **Reimbursing credit card** — the universal $0 path for veterans; TSA's official list also covers **Global Entry ($120, includes PreCheck)** — the better buy if you ever fly internationally.
- **Pick your provider deliberately** — IDEMIA is cheapest new ($76.75) and cheapest online renewal ($58.75); Telos is cheapest in-person renewal ($58.75).
- **Enroll before separating** if you can't use a waiver — you'll have a seamless KTN transition (your DoD ID stops working the day you're out).
- **Kids ride along free** — children 17 and under join an enrolled parent in the PreCheck lane on the same boarding pass.

### FAQ

#### Do veterans get free TSA PreCheck?
Most don't — TSA's benefit ends when you leave DoD, and the standard $76.75–$85 fee applies. Exception: veterans enrolled in VA healthcare with service-connected permanent blindness, or who require a VA-issued wheelchair or prosthetic limb, enroll free (including renewals) under the VETS Safe Travel Act of January 2025.

#### How do active-duty military get TSA PreCheck for free?
Enter the 10-digit DoD ID number from the back of your CAC as the Known Traveler Number in every reservation (and your DTS/frequent-flyer profiles). It works for personal travel too. There's no application and no card to show — the indicator must print on your boarding pass.

#### Does TSA PreCheck for military cover the National Guard and Reserves?
Yes — active duty, Reserves, Army and Air National Guard, Coast Guard and its Reserve, academy cadets/midshipmen, and USPHS/NOAA commissioned officers are all included, per TSA.

#### Do military spouses get a TSA PreCheck discount?
Yes — $25 off new enrollment or renewal at all three providers (CLEAR, IDEMIA, Telos), with a USID card showing "Spouse" and an Active Duty/Reserve/Guard sponsor. Spouses of veterans are not eligible.

#### Do Gold Star families get TSA PreCheck free?
Yes — military survivor families (Gold Star lapel, Next of Kin Lapel Button, or families of members who died of a service-connected injury/illness after separation) enroll free using a TAPS verification letter or TSA acknowledgement form. Each family member needs their own letter.

#### Which disabled veterans qualify for free enrollment?
Per tsa.gov: veterans enrolled in VA healthcare whose service-connected disability is permanent blindness, or who require a VA-issued wheelchair or prosthetic limb. Download the fee-waiver letter from VA.gov and enroll through IDEMIA only.

#### Does my TSA PreCheck keep working after I leave the military?
No. TSA states benefits "do not continue after leaving DOD." Enroll as a civilian (or use the disability waiver / a reimbursing credit card) to keep PreCheck.

#### How much does TSA PreCheck cost in 2026?
New enrollment: $76.75 (IDEMIA), $79.95 (CLEAR), $85.00 (Telos). Online renewal: $58.75 (IDEMIA), $69.95 (CLEAR/Telos). Membership lasts 5 years. As of July 14, 2026.

#### What's the cheapest way for a veteran to get PreCheck?
In order: the VETS Safe disability waiver (free, if you qualify) → a credit card that reimburses the fee (effectively free; TSA lists them) → IDEMIA at $76.75. If you fly internationally, put the reimbursement toward Global Entry ($120) instead — it includes PreCheck.

#### Is there a Veterans Day sale on TSA PreCheck?
No. Fees are set by federal rule and never go on sale; any site advertising a PreCheck holiday discount code is fabricating it.
