## Headline finding

**Yes — the Amex Platinum is effectively free for active-duty servicemembers and their spouses, and this is the single best-known credit-card benefit in the military community.** Under the **MLA**, American Express adjusts the annual fee to **$0 on personal cards opened while you are a covered borrower** (active duty; Guard/Reserve on 30+ day orders; spouses/dependents). It is **automatic** — Amex checks Department of Defense records at application; there is no code and no enrollment. If you held the Platinum **before** going on active duty, the **SCRA** applies instead: a 6% interest cap whose definition of "interest" includes **annual membership fees, late fees, and returned-payment fees** — so the fee is waived/refunded for the active-duty period, but you **must request it** (online, or 1-866-391-1460) while on active duty or within 180 days after.

The honest limits, stated plainly: **veterans and retirees not on active duty get no ongoing waiver** — outside the 180-day SCRA window the Platinum costs the full $895. **Business/corporate Platinum cards** don't get the MLA $0-fee adjustment. And the fee waiver never touches the card's earnings: welcome offer, Membership Rewards points, lounge access, and statement credits all work normally.

Archetype: **regulated-benefit / statutory-relief brand.** The "discount" is compliance with federal law; eligibility is legally defined, not a marketing choice; the make-or-break variable is the **account-open date** (before vs. during active duty → SCRA vs. MLA).

## Stacking Rules (verified)
1. **SCRA and MLA never stack on one account** — the account-open date picks exactly one. "SCRA relief is available only on accounts opened… prior to active military duty"; MLA covers accounts opened during coverage. *(Amex SCRA & MLA FAQs)*
2. **MLA is automatic; SCRA must be requested.** Never tell readers SCRA self-applies. *(Amex SCRA & MLA FAQs)*
3. **The fee waiver does not reduce the card's earnings** — welcome offer, Membership Rewards, credits, and lounge access are unaffected and work normally.
4. **The waiver covers the fee, not spending** — there's no percent-off at merchants and no code; nothing to stack with portals or promos.
5. **Business/corporate Platinum cards get no MLA fee adjustment** (consumer credit only); SCRA's rate cap can still reach a pre-service business/corporate account.
6. **Per-account coverage:** each covered borrower's own personal cards are covered — which is why two spouses can hold two fee-waived Platinums, and why one member can hold Platinum + Gold + Delta Reserve etc., all at $0, while covered.

### Intro
The American Express Platinum Card's $895 annual fee drops to **$0** for active-duty servicemembers and their spouses — not through a discount code, but under federal law. If you open the card while you're covered by the **Military Lending Act**, Amex waives the fee **automatically**: it checks Department of Defense records at application, and the $0 fee is applied the moment the account opens.

Already had the Platinum before going on active duty? Then the **Servicemembers Civil Relief Act** is your route — it caps interest at 6% and, because Amex counts annual fees as "interest" under SCRA, waives the fee for your active-duty period. Unlike MLA, you have to **request** it. Veterans and retirees no longer on active duty get no ongoing waiver — that's the honest fine print most pages skip.

### Who Qualifies
- **Active-duty members** of the Army, Navy, Air Force, Marine Corps, Coast Guard (and activated Space Force personnel under the Armed Forces umbrella) — MLA if the account opens during service; SCRA if it predates service.
- **National Guard / Reserve on active orders of more than 30 days** — MLA covered borrowers while activated.
- **Military spouses and dependents** (DEERS-listed; more than half support) — MLA $0-fee relief on their **own** accounts.
- **Veterans / military retirees not on active duty** — **no ongoing waiver**; the SCRA claim window closes 180 days after leaving active duty.
- **Business and corporate Platinum cards** — excluded from the MLA fee adjustment.

### How To Redeem — MLA (new card while serving)
1. Apply for the **personal** Platinum Card on americanexpress.com as normal — there is no special military application page or code.
2. Amex screens the application against Department of Defense MLA records automatically. (Check your own status first at mla.dmdc.osd.mil if unsure.)
3. If you're a covered borrower, the annual fee is **adjusted to $0 automatically** as soon as the account is opened. Your Card Member Agreement (Part 1) will identify you as a covered borrower.

### How To Redeem — SCRA (card opened before active duty)
1. Log in → Account Services → Card Management → **"Servicemembers Civil Relief Act (SCRA) Benefits"** (fastest), or call **1-866-391-1460** (7am–11:30pm, 7 days), fax 623-444-3000, or mail PO Box 981535, El Paso, TX 79998-1535.
2. Provide military documentation: active-duty orders, change-of-station orders, a DD-214, or a commanding-officer letter.
3. Amex applies the 6% cap + fee waiver **within 2 billing cycles** and sends a confirmation letter.
4. File **while on active duty or within 180 days** of completing it.

### Exclusions And Fine Print
- No retail discount, coupon, or code exists — the benefit is the fee waiver itself.
- One relief per account, decided by the open date; they never stack.
- Veterans/retirees outside the 180-day window: standard $895 terms.
- Business/corporate cards: no MLA fee adjustment.
- Amex can change the fee and credit lineup at any time (it did in September 2025).

### Best Ways To Save More
- **Both spouses apply separately** — each covered borrower gets their own $0-fee Platinum and their own welcome offer; don't settle for authorized-user status.
- **Stack the card family:** MLA applies per personal card — Gold, Delta Reserve, Hilton Aspire etc. can all sit at $0 while you're covered.
- **Use the credits** — the fee is $0 but the up-to-$600 hotel, $200 airline, $200 Uber Cash, $300 digital-entertainment and other credits still work (most require enrollment).
- **File SCRA on old accounts** — pre-service accounts can yield historical fee refunds.
- **Compare issuers:** Chase, USAA, Navy Federal and Citi also honor SCRA/MLA; Amex stands out for applying $0 to its most premium cards.
