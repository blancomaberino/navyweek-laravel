# Microsoft 365 / Office — Military Discount & Maximum-Savings Brief

**Researched:** July 6, 2026 · **Baseline cart for all math:** one year of **Microsoft 365 Family** (standard estimated retail ≈ $99.99/yr for the reference edition; **verify live** — Microsoft prices M365 Family at its regular rate at retail).

## Headline finding

Microsoft runs **two distinct, officially documented military offers** — and the bigger one has an important catch. The standout is the **Microsoft 365 Family Military Appreciation Edition: "Save 30 percent"**, sold for a flat annual price described on Microsoft's own page as **$99.99/year** — **but you buy it "from your Exchange"** (AAFES/NEX/etc.), not on Microsoft.com. The page routes Army/Air Force/Navy buyers to their Exchange, so this is an **Exchange-channel** benefit tied to your ability to shop the military Exchange, not a self-serve web discount. Separately, the **Microsoft Store gives active, former, and retired military (and their families) up to 10% off select products** — but that 10% explicitly **excludes digital subscriptions, apps, and services** (it's aimed at Surface/hardware). So the honest headline: *the best Microsoft 365 deal for the military is the 30%-off Family "Military Appreciation Edition" through the Exchange — the 10% Microsoft Store discount does not apply to Microsoft 365 subscriptions.* The old **Home Use Program** is retired; its successor is the **Workplace Discount Program**, which is an **employer/organization benefit**, not a general military discount. Archetype: **channel-gated software discount — which store you buy from decides your price.**

> **Debunk:** Third-party posts blur three separate things into "the Microsoft military discount." (1) The **30% M365 Family** deal is real but is an **Exchange-channel** benefit ($99.99/yr, "buy from your Exchange") — not a code you enter on Microsoft.com. (2) The **10% Microsoft Store** discount is real but is for **hardware/select products** and, per its own terms, **doesn't apply to subscriptions/apps/services** — so it does **not** discount a Microsoft 365 subscription. (3) The "30% via the Home Use Program" advice is **stale** — HUP was replaced by the **Workplace Discount Program**, which is an employer benefit requiring a qualifying work email, not a military entitlement. Don't tell a veteran to "use HUP."

Microsoft offers the military community two separate, officially documented deals — and the one that actually discounts your Microsoft 365 subscription is the **Microsoft 365 Family Military Appreciation Edition**. ### How To Redeem
**M365 Family Military Appreciation Edition (the subscription deal):**
1. Go to Microsoft's Microsoft 365 military page.
2. Select your branch (US Army, US Air Force, or US Navy) — the link routes you to **your Exchange**.
3. Purchase the **Military Appreciation Edition** at your Exchange (~$99.99/yr, 30% off).

**Microsoft Store 10% (hardware):**
1. Shop eligible Surface/laptop/tablet products at the Microsoft Store military page.
2. Chat with a Microsoft Store agent or visit a store to confirm the military discount is applied; review the Student & Military Discount Terms.

### How Verification Works
For the **Store 10%**, Microsoft applies the discount through the Microsoft Store (online chat/agent or in person) under its Student & Military Discount Terms. For the **Exchange 30% edition**, your eligibility is effectively your **Exchange shopping access** — the purchase happens on the Exchange, which already verifies military affiliation. Microsoft does not use ID.me/SheerID publicly for these military offers.

- **Store 10%** applies to "select products" (hardware); per its terms it **excludes subscriptions, apps, and services** — so it won't discount Microsoft 365.

- The **30% Family edition** is an **Exchange purchase** — pricing and fulfillment are set through the Exchange; "actual reseller pricing and savings may vary."
- **Home Use Program is retired**; the Workplace Discount Program requires a **participating employer** and work email — a .mil address alone doesn't qualify.

### FAQ

#### Does Microsoft offer a military discount?
Yes — two: a **30%-off Microsoft 365 Family "Military Appreciation Edition"** sold through your **military Exchange** (~$99.99/yr), and **up to 10% off select products** (mainly hardware) at the Microsoft Store.

#### How do I get 30% off Microsoft 365 as a service member?
Buy the **M365 Family Military Appreciation Edition from your Exchange** (AAFES/NEX). Microsoft's military page routes Army/Air Force/Navy buyers to their Exchange to purchase it.

#### Does the Microsoft Store 10% military discount apply to Microsoft 365?
No. The 10% is for **select products/hardware** and, per its terms, **excludes subscriptions, apps, and services** — so it won't discount an M365 subscription.

#### Is the Home Use Program still available?
No. **HUP is retired.** Its successor, the **Workplace Discount Program**, is an **employer benefit** — you need a participating organization and a work email, not just military status.

#### Do veterans qualify?
For the **Store 10%**, yes — it covers "active, former, and retired military" and families. For the **Exchange 30% edition**, eligibility depends on your **Exchange shopping access**.

#### Does Microsoft use ID.me or GovX for military verification?
Not for these offers. The Store 10% is applied at the Microsoft Store under its military terms; the 30% edition is bought through the Exchange, which handles military affiliation.

#### Can I combine the 10% with a sale?
No — the Store discount "may not be combined with any other offer."

#### What if I'm a student or teacher?
Use Microsoft's **education pricing** — it's a separate (often cheaper) route than either military offer.
