## Headline finding

**Yes — by consistent reporting, the Chase Sapphire Reserve is effectively free for active-duty servicemembers and their spouses, making the most expensive mainstream travel card ($795/yr, raised from $550 in the June 2025 relaunch) one of the best deals in the military community.** Under the **MLA**, Chase checks applicants against Department of Defense records at account opening and adjusts the annual fee to **$0 on personal cards opened while you're a covered borrower** (active duty; Guard/Reserve on 30+ day orders; spouses/dependents) — **automatic**, no code, no enrollment; Chase mails a benefits letter as confirmation. Editors report the **$195 authorized-user fee is waived too**. If you held the card **before** activating, the **SCRA** applies instead — Chase is reported to reduce eligible balances to **4% APR** (below the 6% statutory cap) and waive/credit fees, by **request** (secure message, mail, or 1-877-469-0110).

The honest limits: **veterans and retirees not on active duty get no ongoing waiver** — community reports say Chase re-checks MLA status after separation and the fee returns within roughly 1–2 years (spouses report verification letters 6–9 months out); never promise a grace period. **Business cards are excluded.** And the waiver never touches the card's earnings: the welcome offer (100,000 points as of July 19, 2026), 8x Chase Travel points, the $300 travel credit, $500 The Edit credit, $300 dining, $300 StubHub, Apple TV+/Music, DoorDash, Lyft, lounge access, and the Global Entry credit all work normally — a fee-waived cardholder keeps every dollar of it.

Archetype: **regulated-benefit / statutory-relief brand** (dominant; no secondary). The "discount" is compliance with — and reported voluntary extension of — federal law; eligibility is legally defined; the make-or-break variable is the **account-open date** (before vs. during covered service → SCRA vs. MLA).

## Stacking Rules (verified)
1. **SCRA and MLA never stack on one account** — the account-open date picks exactly one. SCRA reaches obligations "incurred… before" service (50 U.S.C. § 3937); MLA governs credit extended to an already-covered borrower.
2. **MLA is automatic; SCRA must be requested.** Chase's own SCRA page documents a request process; the MLA check runs at account opening. Never tell readers SCRA self-applies, and never tell them the MLA waiver needs a request.
3. **The waiver covers the fee, not spending** — no percent-off at merchants, nothing to stack with portals, promos, or shopping codes.
4. **Card earnings are unaffected** — welcome offer, 8x/4x/3x earning, Points Boost, the full credit lineup, and lounge access work normally on a fee-waived card (editorial consensus; no Chase statement to the contrary).
5. **Business cards get no MLA fee waiver** (consumer credit only). A pre-service business account may still be claimable under statutory SCRA — Chase doesn't publish product scope; route readers to 1-877-469-0110.
6. **Per-borrower coverage:** spouses are covered borrowers in their own right — which is why a couple can hold two fee-waived CSRs, and why one member can hold CSR + Sapphire Preferred + co-brands, all at $0, while covered (reported; there's no published card list).

### Who Qualifies
- **Active-duty members** of the Army, Navy, Air Force, Marine Corps, Coast Guard, and Space Force — MLA if the account opens during service; SCRA if it predates active duty.
- **National Guard / Reserve on Title 10 or Title 32 orders of more than 30 days** — MLA covered borrowers while activated.
- **Military spouses and dependents** (DEERS-enrolled) — covered on their **own** cards as primary cardholders; Chase screens by SSN against the same DoD database.
- **Veterans / military retirees not on active duty** — **no ongoing waiver**; the statutory SCRA claim window closes 180 days after release, and community reports say the fee returns within a year or two of separation as Chase re-checks records.
- **Business cards (Ink family)** — excluded from the MLA fee waiver.

### How To Redeem — MLA (new card while serving)
1. Apply for the **personal** Sapphire Reserve on chase.com as normal — there is no military application page and no code.
2. Chase checks your SSN against the Department of Defense MLA database at account opening (self-check at mla.dmdc.osd.mil if unsure; spouses are in the same system via DEERS).
3. If you're a covered borrower, the annual fee is **adjusted to $0 automatically** and Chase **mails a benefits letter** confirming it. Questions → Chase Military Services **1-877-469-0110**.

### How To Redeem — SCRA (card opened before active duty)
1. Log in at chase.com/securemessages → New Message → choose **"Servicemembers Civil Relief Act (SCRA)"** from the dropdown and attach electronic copies of your active-duty orders.
2. Or mail: Chase, Attn: SCRA Request, PO Box 183240, Columbus, OH 43218-3240. Or call **1-877-469-0110** (M–F 8am–9pm ET; **1-318-340-3308** overseas).
3. Reported result: APR reduced to **4%** on eligible balances and fees (including the annual fee) waived/credited, through active duty and up to one year after. Statutory floor regardless: 6% cap with excess interest forgiven.
4. File **while on active duty or within 180 days** of release.

### Exclusions And Fine Print
- No retail discount, coupon, or code exists — the benefit is the fee waiver itself.
- One relief per account, decided by the open date; MLA and SCRA never stack.
- Veterans/retirees outside active duty: standard $795 terms; expect the fee to return after separation (reports: within ~1–2 years).
- Business cards: no MLA fee waiver.
- The **$0-fee, 4%, and +1-year figures are Chase practice as reported, not published chase.com terms** — confirm on 1-877-469-0110.
- Chase changed the card materially in June 2025 (fee $550 → $795, new credit lineup); it can change terms again at any time.
