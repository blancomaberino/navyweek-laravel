# Spirit Airlines — Military Discount & Maximum-Savings Brief

**Researched:** July 14, 2026 · **Baseline cart for all math:** one domestic round-trip for an active-duty service member with 2 checked bags + 1 carry-on (the cart where Spirit's benefit used to matter — on an ultra-low-cost carrier, bags routinely added $120–$240 round-trip).

## Headline finding

**Spirit Airlines no longer exists as an operating airline.** It ceased all flights effective May 2, 2026 and is in a court-supervised wind-down after its second Chapter 11 bankruptcy — so its well-known military benefit (two free checked bags, free carry-on, and priority boarding for active-duty members and accompanying spouses/children, verified via ID.me) is **defunct**. Spirit never discounted fares for military; the benefit was always bags and boarding. The page's job now is to correct the record — aggregator pages still advertising the "Spirit military discount" are stale — and route military travelers to the airlines that still fly, all of which (per the DOT customer-service dashboard) commit to **at least one free carry-on and two free checked bags for service members**. Archetype: **H (the "myth"/correction page)** — here because the entire brand, not just the discount, is gone.

## Stacking Rules (verified where sources allow)
1. **There is nothing to stack at Spirit — the airline is defunct.** Do not attempt to book; customer service no longer exists.
2. **At surviving airlines, the military baggage waiver applies automatically with a valid military ID** — it layers on any fare, including sale fares, because it's a fee waiver, not a fare discount.
3. **Military fare discounts are essentially extinct industry-wide** — compare total trip cost (fare + fees) rather than hunting a fare code.
4. **Orders vs leisure matters** at mainline carriers: the 4–5-bag allowances typically require travel on orders; personal travel gets 2–3 bags. Never claim the orders allowance for leisure travel.

### Who Qualifies
- Nobody — the airline no longer operates. Historically: active-duty U.S. military plus accompanying spouse/children (bags + boarding); veterans and non-accompanying dependents were **not** covered.

### What To Do If You Hold A Spirit Ticket
1. Do not go to the airport — all flights are cancelled and customer service is closed.
2. If you paid by credit card, dispute the charge with your card issuer for undelivered service.
3. Watch the bankruptcy wind-down (Epiq case site for Spirit Aviation Holdings) for claim instructions; refunds route through the case, not spirit.com.
4. Book replacement travel on a surviving carrier — the DOT-coordinated discounted rescue fares from May 2026 have ended.

### How The Benefit Used To Work (historical record)
Active-duty members selected "Active Duty U.S. Military Personnel" during booking and verified through **ID.me** (or showed a military ID at the airport); each qualifying traveler got two free checked bags and a free carry-on, and accompanying spouses/children added their free bags with an agent at the ticket counter. Complimentary priority boarding required presenting a military ID at the gate. This is documented history, not a current offer.

### Exclusions And Fine Print
- The Spirit benefit was **active duty only** (plus accompanying spouse/children) — veterans were never included.
- It was never a fare discount.
- All of it ended May 2, 2026.

### Best Ways To Save More (surviving alternatives)
- **Frontier:** active-duty members get 2 free checked bags with oversize/overweight fees waived on them — the closest ULCC substitute for Spirit's formula.
- **American / Delta / United:** 2–3 free bags on personal travel, up to 5 (heavier limits) on orders, with military ID.
- **Southwest:** 2 free checked bags policy.
- **DOT floor:** whichever dashboard airline is cheapest that day still owes a service member ≥1 free carry-on + ≥2 free checked bags with military ID.
- **On orders?** Always say so — the orders allowance is dramatically larger at mainline carriers.

### FAQ
#### Does Spirit Airlines offer a military discount?
No — and not because of policy: Spirit Airlines ceased all operations on May 2, 2026 and is being wound down in bankruptcy. Its former military baggage-and-boarding benefit no longer exists.

#### What was the Spirit Airlines military benefit?
Historically, active-duty U.S. service members and accompanying spouses/children each received two free checked bags and a free carry-on plus complimentary priority boarding, verified via ID.me or a military ID. Spirit never discounted fares.

#### Did veterans qualify for Spirit's military benefit?
No. The benefit was limited to active-duty members and their accompanying spouse/children.

#### I still have a Spirit ticket — how do I get my money back?
Spirit's customer service is closed. Dispute the charge with your credit-card issuer and monitor the bankruptcy wind-down (Spirit Aviation Holdings case) for claim procedures.

#### Which airlines give military free checked bags now?
All airlines on the DOT customer-service dashboard commit to at least one free carry-on and two free checked bags for service members with military ID. Frontier waives bag fees for active duty; American, Delta, and United allow up to five free bags on orders.

#### What's the cheapest way for a service member to fly after Spirit's shutdown?
Compare total cost (fare + fees) across carriers. Frontier's low base fares plus its active-duty bag waiver most closely replicates the old Spirit deal; Southwest's everyone-gets-bags policy is the simplest.

#### Could the Spirit brand come back?
Spirit's remaining assets (including brand assets) are being auctioned in the wind-down. If a buyer revives the brand, any military benefit would be new and separate — nothing carries over. As of July 14, 2026, Spirit is not flying.
