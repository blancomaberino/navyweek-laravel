# Bank of America — Military Benefits (SCRA/MLA) & Fee-Relief Brief

**Researched:** July 19, 2026 · **Baseline "cart" for all math:** one Bank of America Premium Rewards Elite credit card with a **$550** annual fee (the card the "does BofA waive fees for military" question is really about), plus a pre-service card balance for the SCRA rate-cap math.

> **This is a statutory-relief page, not a retail percent-off discount — and its honest story is INCONSISTENCY.** Bank of America has no "military discount," and its Military Lending Act fee handling is the **least consistent among the major issuers**. Amex, Chase, and Citi are reported to zero annual fees on *all* personal cards for MLA-covered borrowers; Capital One waives none. Bank of America sits in the murky middle: **no published MLA fee policy anywhere on bankofamerica.com**, community reports of an unofficial **"only fees over $100" threshold**, a $550 card that is *usually* — but not always fully — credited, and sub-$100 cards that are simply charged. The page's job is to say exactly what is and isn't reliable, and to tell readers: **open a BofA card only if you're prepared to pay the annual fee.**

## Headline finding

There is **no promo code and no retail discount.** Bank of America's military offering splits into three tiers of reliability:

1. **Reliable and official — SCRA (accounts opened *before* active duty):** BofA publishes a full SCRA program at bankofamerica.com/military-banking/: the statutory **6% interest cap** on pre-service credit cards, lines of credit, and installment loans for the entire active-duty period (**4%** on home loans BofA both owns and services — the one place BofA beats the statute with a number), plus deposit-account fee waivers (monthly maintenance, ATM, wire, debit-replacement, check-order fees). BofA also states it **"generally extends" credit-card and installment-loan benefits for 6 months after active duty ends**, and — unusually — advertises "continued select credit card fees waived **for life** after military service" (its words; no fee list or amounts published). Benefits are **requested**, not automatic: online portal, phone **877.345.0693**, fax, or mail, within **180 days** of completing service.
2. **Real but unofficial and inconsistent — MLA (cards opened *during* covered service):** BofA runs applicants against DoD records and issues an **"MLA CREDIT – ANNUAL FEE"** statement credit on some cards — but it publishes **nothing** about this. Community data points (2021–2026) converge on an unofficial pattern: fees are credited **only on cards with annual fees over ~$100**. The **$550 Premium Rewards Elite** is usually fully credited (charged, then refunded at statement close, no request needed), but at least one cardholder got only **$450 credited, leaving a $100 fee**. The **$95 Premium Rewards** and **$95 Alaska Airlines** cards are **not** waived (an Alaska denial is documented from January 2022, when that fee was $75). This is lawful: 32 CFR 232.4(d) lets issuers exclude bona fide, reasonable annual fees from the 36% MAPR — waiving is voluntary, and BofA volunteers less than its peers.
3. **Not covered:** veterans and retirees not on active duty get no ongoing card-fee relief (beyond the vague "select fees waived for life" line, which BofA does not enumerate); **state** National Guard duty under a governor's orders is explicitly ineligible — federal orders only.

Archetype: **regulated-benefit / statutory-relief brand** (same family as American Express, Chase, Citi, Capital One). The make-or-break variable is the **account-open date vs. active-duty start date** — but the BofA twist is a second variable no other major issuer has: **the size of the annual fee** (over ~$100 = reportedly credited; under = charged).

## Stacking Rules (verified)
1. **SCRA vs. MLA never stack on one account** — the account-open date picks exactly one. SCRA reaches pre-service obligations (50 U.S.C. § 3937); MLA governs credit extended to an already-covered borrower, and card accounts only from October 3, 2017 (32 CFR 232.13).
2. **SCRA must be requested** (portal, phone, fax, or mail with orders or the CO-signed form; 180-day post-service deadline). **The MLA credit, where it happens, is reported automatic** — no request channel exists, because no program is published. Never tell readers to "call and ask for the MLA waiver"; community consensus is the opposite ("do not call, just wait until the AF hits").
3. **The fee-size threshold is the stack-breaker unique to BofA:** over ~$100 = reportedly credited; under = charged. This is practice, not policy, and BofA can change it silently in either direction.
4. **Fee relief ≠ rewards change** — points, welcome bonuses, and the PRE's $300/$150/$120 credits are unaffected by any MLA/SCRA relief; there is nothing retail to stack.
5. **Spouse/dependent coverage is broader than statute at BofA (SCRA side):** its form extends interest-rate benefits to a requesting spouse, domestic partner, or dependent "regardless of whether the Servicemember is liable on the account." On the MLA side, spouses/dependents are covered borrowers by law (32 CFR 232), and one dependent reports receiving the full PRE credit.
6. **Business cards:** the MLA covers consumer credit only — no fee relief on business cards under MLA anywhere, BofA included. (Pre-service business obligations may still have SCRA claims; route to 877.345.0693.)

Its **SCRA program is real and published**: a 6% interest cap on accounts you opened before active duty (4% on a mortgage BofA owns and services), waived checking and ATM fees, and benefits that generally run six months past your active-duty end date on credit cards.

If a guaranteed $0-fee premium card is what you want, Amex and Chase publish and honor that deal — Bank of America doesn't promise it, so **open a BofA card only if you're prepared to pay the fee.**

### Who Qualifies
- **Federal active duty** (Army, Navy, Air Force, Marine Corps, Coast Guard — plus PHS/NOAA commissioned officers per BofA's form) — SCRA on pre-service accounts; MLA coverage on during-service cards.
- **National Guard / Reserve on federal active-duty orders** — eligible; BofA's FAQ is explicit that **governor-ordered state duty is not** (some states have their own laws — check yours).
- **Spouses, domestic partners, dependents** — BofA's form extends SCRA interest-rate benefits to a requesting spouse/domestic partner/dependent "regardless of whether the Servicemember is liable on the account"; under MLA they are covered borrowers on their own applications. A spouse can request benefits but may need power of attorney for account decisions.
- **Veterans / retirees not on active duty** — no ongoing card relief. Two tails exist: the 180-day post-service SCRA request window, and BofA's published-but-unenumerated "select credit card fees waived for life after military service" — call 877.345.0693 to ask what that covers; don't count on it.
- **Business cards** — no MLA fee relief (consumer credit only).

### How To Request SCRA Benefits
1. Gather your **active-duty orders** — or BofA's **Military Orders Detail form** signed by you and your commanding officer (accepted as a substitute).
2. Apply online via the **Request Benefits** portal at bankofamerica.com/military-banking/, or call the **Military Benefits Unit at 877.345.0693** (Mon–Fri 9 a.m.–8 p.m.; collect from overseas 817.245.4094).
3. Or fax **866.696.0292** / mail: Military Benefits Unit, PO Box 673026, Dallas, TX 75267-3026 (overnight: Dept FL96000297, 9000 Southside Blvd., Jacksonville, FL 32256). Email contact: militarybenefits@bankofamerica.com (avoid sending documents unsecured).
4. File **during active duty or within 180 days** of completing service. Benefits cover your active-duty period; BofA generally extends card/installment benefits ~6 months past it, and home-loan benefits 12 months.

### How The MLA Credit Works (what's reported — BofA publishes nothing)
1. Apply for the personal card as normal — there is no military application, code, or request form.
2. BofA screens applicants against DoD records; its card agreements acknowledge "credits made in accordance with… the Military Lending Act" without stating any policy.
3. Per cardholder reports: on cards with fees **over ~$100**, the annual fee posts and an **"MLA CREDIT – ANNUAL FEE"** appears around statement close — automatically. On $95-and-under cards, no credit comes.
4. If the credit doesn't appear, the dedicated **MLA line is 844.441.9168** (collect 302.318.8745) — but there is no published policy to point to, so treat the fee as yours to pay.

### Exclusions And Fine Print
- **No published MLA fee-waiver policy exists** — every figure in the MLA section above is cardholder-reported practice, not a BofA commitment; it can change or vary by account without notice.
- **Accounts opened before October 3, 2017 can never get MLA relief** (the MLA's credit-card provisions began that day) — they're SCRA territory only.
- **SCRA covers pre-service obligations only**; during-service accounts fail the statutory test (denial letters confirm BofA applies this).
- **State-Guard governor call-ups are ineligible** for BofA's federal SCRA benefits.
- The "select credit card fees waived for life after military service" line is official BofA language with **no published list of fees** — confirm by phone before relying on it.
- Bona-fide insurance and lawful bona fide fees sit outside the SCRA/MLA caps (50 USC 3937; 32 CFR 232.4(d)).
- Rewards, credits, and welcome offers are unaffected by any relief — this is fee/rate law, not a rewards program.

### Best Ways To Save More

- **If you're chasing the PRE anyway:** its $300 airline + $150 lifestyle credits offset the fee for heavy travelers even unwaived — run that math as a civilian would, then treat any MLA credit as a bonus.

#### Does Bank of America offer a military discount?
No retail discount. BofA honors the SCRA — a **6% interest cap** on accounts opened before active duty (4% on home loans it owns and services), plus deposit-account fee waivers — and is reported (but does not promise) to credit annual fees over ~$100 on cards opened during service under the MLA.

#### Does Bank of America waive credit card annual fees for military?
**Sometimes — and it publishes no policy.** Cardholder reports since 2021: fees **over ~$100** (like the $550 Premium Rewards Elite) usually auto-credit as "MLA CREDIT – ANNUAL FEE"; the **$95 Premium Rewards and $95 Alaska Airlines fees are not waived**; one PRE holder got only $450 of $550 back. Plan to pay the fee; treat a credit as upside.

#### Is the Premium Rewards Elite free for active-duty military?
Usually, per community reports — the $550 fee posts and is credited at statement close for MLA-covered borrowers, automatically. But BofA doesn't publish this, and at least one report shows a $450 partial credit leaving $100. It is not a guarantee like Amex's or Chase's.

#### Why doesn't Bank of America waive all fees like Amex and Chase?
Because it doesn't have to. The MLA's rules (32 CFR 232.4(d)) exclude bona fide, reasonable annual fees from the 36% MAPR cap — so zeroing fees is voluntary. Amex, Chase, and (reportedly) Citi volunteer; Capital One doesn't; BofA volunteers partially and unofficially.

#### What is Bank of America's SCRA benefit?
On accounts opened **before** active duty: interest capped at **6%** for your whole active-duty period (fees count as interest under the statute, and excess interest is forgiven, not deferred). Home loans BofA owns and services get **4%**, with benefits extending 12 months past service; credit cards and installment loans "generally" get a 6-month extension.

#### How do I request SCRA benefits from Bank of America?
Online through the Request Benefits portal at bankofamerica.com/military-banking/, by phone at **877.345.0693**, by fax (866.696.0292), or by mail (Military Benefits Unit, PO Box 673026, Dallas, TX 75267-3026) — with your orders or BofA's Military Orders Detail form signed by your commanding officer. File during active duty or within **180 days** after.

#### Do spouses and dependents qualify?
Yes, more broadly than at most issuers on the SCRA side: BofA's own form extends interest-rate benefits to a requesting spouse, domestic partner, or dependent "regardless of whether the Servicemember is liable on the account." Under the MLA, spouses/dependents are covered borrowers on their own cards, and one dependent reports receiving the full PRE credit.

#### I'm in the National Guard — am I covered?
Only on **federal** orders from the President or Secretary of Defense. BofA's FAQ says governor-ordered state duty is not eligible (your state's own law may help — check it).

#### Do veterans get anything after leaving service?
Cards/installment loans: benefits generally run ~6 more months, and you have 180 days post-service to file SCRA claims. BofA also advertises "continued select credit card fees waived for life after military service" — official words with no published details; call 877.345.0693 and ask what it covers before counting on it.

#### Does Bank of America use ID.me, SheerID, or GovX?
No. MLA screening runs on Department of Defense records; SCRA runs on your orders or the CO-signed form, reviewed by BofA's Military Benefits Unit.

#### What's actually the best move for a servicemember?
Pre-service BofA accounts → file SCRA now. Everyday banking → take the published deposit-fee waivers. A new premium card during service → BofA is the inconsistent option; if you need the fee waived *for certain*, open with Amex or Chase instead, and treat any BofA MLA credit as a pleasant surprise.
