# Specialized — Military Discount & Maximum-Savings Brief

**Researched:** July 10, 2026 · **Baseline cart for all math:** one **Specialized Sirrus 2.0** fitness/hybrid bike — **$774.99 MSRP**, pre-tax (the price on specialized.com; a mainstream, mid-priced Specialized purchase). Equipment math uses a **$150 helmet** as a secondary reference where the 30%-off-equipment tier matters.

## Headline finding

Specialized (specialized.com) runs a **real, brand-operated identity discount — the "Hero Pro Program" — verified through ID.me**. Specialized states it on its own Hero Pro Program page: it has "partnered with **ID.me** to provide **Active Military, Military Spouses and Dependents, Veterans, First Responders, Nurses and Teachers**" access to a members-only portal. The exact per-order percentage is **gated behind the ID.me login**, but ID.me's own Specialized store page publishes the public figure: **15%–30% off bikes and 30% off equipment** for military members, spouses, dependents, veterans, first responders, nurses, and teachers. That's the honest everyday answer and, for someone buying a full-price Specialized bike, the single best standing route.

The nuance: Specialized is a **premium brand that rarely discounts new-model bikes publicly**, so the Hero Pro percentage is usually the ceiling on a *current* bike — but it is **not** always the best price overall. The one thing that can beat it is a **model-year closeout / clearance** (outgoing colors and last-year bikes get marked down at Specialized's own Sale section and at dealers, sometimes 30–40%+), and those closeout prices generally **won't stack** with the Hero Pro discount. Archetype: **dominant A — verification-gated identity discount (ID.me Hero Pro portal)**; secondary **E-flavor — premium brand with almost no public-code game**, so the standing verified discount is the everyday floor and only a genuine closeout beats it. Do **not** repeat the aggregator claims of "20–40%," "40% first-responder," or "55–75% off" military codes — those conflate dealer clearance and generic ID.me marketing with the actual Hero Pro terms, and there is **no ID.me-independent coupon code** for the discount.

## Stacking Rules (verified as far as sources allow)
1. **The Hero Pro discount is a single, gated membership price** — neither Specialized's page nor ID.me states it stacks with public promo codes, the email-signup offer, or advertised sales. Treat it as **one order-level discount** and confirm inside the portal.
2. **Hero Pro vs. closeout/clearance are effectively mutually exclusive** — the portal discounts full-price product; whether it further reduces an already-marked-down closeout bike is **unconfirmed** — verify in the portal, and if not, take whichever public/closeout price is lower.
3. **There is no standalone coupon code** for the military discount — anyone selling a "Specialized military code" outside ID.me is not describing the real program.
4. **The Specialized Pro Program / ExpertVoice pricing is a separate, industry-only channel** — it does not stack with, and is not the same as, the Hero Pro Program.
5. **Affirm financing is not a discount** — 0% APR changes cash flow, not the price; it can apply on top of any price but saves nothing on the total.
6. **Cashback:** unverified this pass; an ID.me-gated portal checkout typically breaks portal tracking — verify a live rate before relying on it.

#### Does Specialized offer a military discount?

#### How much is the Specialized military discount?
The published figure is **15%–30% off bikes and 30% off equipment**. The exact tier for your order is revealed inside the ID.me-gated Hero Pro portal. Coupon sites advertising "20–40%" or "$10 off" military codes are not the verified program.

#### Do veterans, spouses, and dependents qualify?
Yes. Specialized names "**Veterans**" and "**Military Spouses and Dependents**" as eligible, verified through ID.me.

#### How do I verify my status?
Create a free **ID.me** account, verify your eligibility (military, first responder, nurse, or teacher) on the Hero Pro page, and the discounted prices appear in the membership portal.

#### Does Specialized use ID.me, GovX, SheerID, or WeSalute?
The named verification partner is **ID.me**. There is **no confirmed GovX, SheerID, or WeSalute** route for Specialized — treat those aggregator claims as unverified. (GovX carries competitor e-bike brands, not Specialized.)

#### Can I use the discount in stores or only online?
The verified discount is an **online** Hero Pro portal price on specialized.com after ID.me sign-in. In-store discounting at independent dealers isn't part of this program — ask the specific shop.

#### What is excluded?
It's a single, non-combinable gated price with no separate coupon. Returns run 30 days on specialized.com in new/unused condition; crash/impact-damaged product isn't returnable.

#### Can I combine the discount with promo codes or sale items?
No — treat the Hero Pro price as one gated discount. Because Specialized rarely marks down current bikes, Hero Pro is usually your best price; only a genuine model-year closeout (which won't combine) beats it.

#### What's actually the cheapest way for a service member to buy a Specialized bike?
Verify with **ID.me → Hero Pro** for 15%–30% off a current bike (about **$542–$659 on the $774.99 Sirrus 2.0**). If a real closeout on the exact model/size you want is running, compare that public price and buy the lower — the two don't stack.

#### Does Specialized offer a first responder, nurse, or teacher discount?
Yes — the same Hero Pro Program extends to **first responders, nurses, and teachers** through ID.me.

#### Can I buy Specialized through GovX or the Exchange?
No confirmed path this pass — Specialized isn't on GovX (which carries competitor e-bikes) and isn't stocked at the AAFES/NEX exchanges. The military channel is ID.me → Hero Pro.
