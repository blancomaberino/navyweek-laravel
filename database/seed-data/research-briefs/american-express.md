# American Express — Military Benefits & Fee-Waiver Brief

**Researched:** July 15, 2026 · **Baseline "cart" for all math:** one American Express Platinum Card® with an **$895** annual fee (the flagship case shoppers ask about).

> This is a **fee-waiver / rate-relief page, not a retail percent-off discount.** American Express does not run a "military discount" in the coupon sense. What it does — and does famously well — is honor two federal laws, the **Military Lending Act (MLA)** and the **Servicemembers Civil Relief Act (SCRA)**, that cap the cost of consumer credit for active-duty servicemembers. In practice that means **$0 annual fees** on personal Amex cards and a **6% interest cap** on pre-service balances. Frame the page as "how the Amex military fee waiver works," not "what percent do I get off."

## Headline finding

There is **no retail discount** and **no coupon code**. American Express provides two statutory benefits for the military community:

1. **MLA relief (accounts opened *during* active duty):** Amex adjusts the **annual fee to $0** on **personal** cards for MLA "covered borrowers" — active duty, Guard/Reserve on orders of more than 30 days, and their **spouses/dependents**. This is **automatic** — Amex checks the applicant against Department of Defense records at application; no request needed. This is the benefit that lets an eligible servicemember or spouse hold the **$895 Platinum** (or Gold, Delta Reserve, Hilton Aspire, etc.) for **$0/year** while keeping the full card benefits.
2. **SCRA relief (accounts opened *before* active duty):** Amex caps the interest rate on eligible pre-service accounts at **6%**, and its definition of "interest" **includes annual membership fees, late fees, and returned-payment fees** — so those are also effectively waived/refunded for the active-duty period. SCRA is **not automatic**; you must request it and provide documentation, and requests must be made **while on active duty or within 180 days** of leaving active duty.

Archetype: **regulated-benefit / statutory-relief brand** (like a bank or insurer) — the "discount" is compliance with federal law, eligibility is legally defined (not a marketing choice), and the make-or-break variable is **when the account was opened** (before vs. during active duty), which decides SCRA vs. MLA. **Business cards are excluded** from the fee benefit — only personal/consumer cards qualify.

**Verdict logic:**
- **Applying for a new Amex now, and you're active-duty or a military spouse** → do nothing extra; **MLA** zeroes the annual fee automatically at approval.
- **You already had the Amex before deploying / going active** → file an **SCRA** request with your orders; you get the 6% cap and fee waiver, and may recover past fees.
- **You're a veteran no longer on active duty (and >180 days out)** → there is **no ongoing benefit**; the card is standard-priced. (Your pre-service active-duty period may still be claimable within the 180-day window.)
- On-page 1–2 question chooser: *"Did you open the card before or after you went on active duty?"* → before = SCRA (request it); after/while = MLA (automatic).

## Stacking Rules (verified)
1. **SCRA and MLA are mutually exclusive per account** — the account-open date decides which applies. "SCRA relief is available only on accounts opened… prior to active military duty"; MLA applies to accounts opened during active duty. You don't stack both on one card.
2. **MLA relief is automatic and requires no request**; SCRA relief **must be requested with documentation**. Do not tell users SCRA auto-applies.
3. **The $0 fee is not a coupon and does not combine with any promo/percent-off** — none exists. Welcome offers/points on the card are unaffected and continue normally.
4. **Business/corporate cards do not get the MLA $0-fee adjustment** (MLA covers consumer credit only), though SCRA's rate cap can reach a pre-service small-business/corporate account per Amex's stated scope.

### Who Qualifies
- **Active-duty U.S. Armed Forces** (Army, Navy, Air Force, Marine Corps, Coast Guard) — eligible (MLA if account opened during service; SCRA if before).
- **Guard/Reserve on active-duty orders of more than 30 days** — eligible (MLA covered borrower; SCRA if activated and account pre-service).
- **Military spouses / dependents** (>50% financial support) — eligible for **MLA** $0-fee relief; identified via DEERS/DoD records.
- **Veterans not on active duty** — **not** eligible for ongoing relief. SCRA can still be claimed for the active-duty period **within 180 days** of leaving active duty.
- **Business / corporate cards** — **not** eligible for the MLA $0-fee adjustment (consumer cards only).

### How To Redeem — MLA (account opened during active duty)
1. Apply for any **personal** American Express card as normal.
2. Amex checks your SSN against Department of Defense (MLA) records at application. Confirm your own status at mla.dmdc.osd.mil if unsure.
3. If you're a covered borrower, the **annual fee is adjusted to $0 automatically** — no request, no code. Relief is "applied to your account as soon as it is opened."

### How To Redeem — SCRA (account opened before active duty)
1. Log in to your Amex account → Account Services → Card Management → **"Servicemembers Civil Relief Act (SCRA) Benefits."** (Or call **1-866-391-1460**; fax 623-444-3000; or mail.)
2. Upload documentation: active-duty orders, change-of-station orders, DD-214, or a commanding-officer letter.
3. Amex applies relief (6% rate cap + fee waiver, including annual/late/returned-payment fees) within **2 billing cycles**; you receive a confirmation letter.
4. File **while on active duty or within 180 days** of completing active duty.

### How Verification Works
- **MLA:** automatic — Amex matches your application against Department of Defense records (the MLA database). No provider like ID.me or SheerID is involved.
- **SCRA:** manual — you submit military documentation to Amex, which reviews and applies relief. Relief lasts for your active-duty period; Amex may re-verify. Do not assume it is permanent beyond active duty.

### Exclusions And Fine Print
- **No retail discount, coupon, or percent-off exists** — anything claiming a "code" is wrong.
- **SCRA covers pre-service accounts only**; MLA covers accounts opened during active duty. One per account.
- **Business/corporate cards** do not receive the MLA $0-fee benefit.
- SCRA requests **must** be made within active duty or the **180-day** post-service window.
- Card **welcome bonuses, points, and benefits are unaffected** — the fee waiver does not reduce or change them.
- Amex controls the card lineup and fee amounts (e.g., Platinum $895) and can change terms.

### Best Ways To Save More
- **Time your application:** if you're active-duty/spouse and want a premium card, apply **while covered** so MLA zeroes the fee automatically — no premium-card annual fee for the value.
- **File SCRA on older accounts** to claw back fees and cap interest — potential historical refunds on long-held accounts.
- **Hold multiple personal cards:** the $0-fee adjustment applies per personal card for covered borrowers, so premium cards (Gold, Delta Reserve, Hilton Aspire) can be held fee-free while active-duty.
- **Compare issuers:** other issuers (Chase, USAA, Navy Federal, Citi) also waive fees under SCRA/MLA — Amex is notable for applying the $0 fee to its most premium cards.

### FAQ

#### Does American Express offer a military discount?
Not a retail discount. Amex provides **statutory fee and interest relief** under the MLA and SCRA: **$0 annual fees** for active-duty covered borrowers and a **6% interest cap** on pre-service accounts. There is no coupon code or percent-off.

#### How much is the American Express military "discount"?
For covered borrowers, the annual fee is adjusted to **$0** on personal cards — so on an $895 Platinum you save the entire fee each year. Under SCRA, interest on pre-service balances is capped at **6%** and fees (annual, late, returned-payment) are waived.

#### Do veterans, spouses, or dependents qualify?
**Spouses and dependents** qualify for **MLA** $0-fee relief (identified via DoD/DEERS records). **Veterans** not on active duty do **not** get ongoing relief, but can still claim **SCRA** for their active-duty period **within 180 days** of separation.

#### How do I verify my military status?
For **MLA**, you don't — Amex checks Department of Defense records automatically at application. For **SCRA**, you upload documentation (active-duty orders, DD-214, or commanding-officer letter) through your account or by phone/fax/mail.

#### Does American Express use ID.me, GovX, SheerID, or WeSalute?
**No.** Amex verifies through **Department of Defense records** (MLA) and direct document review (SCRA) — no third-party verification provider is used.

#### What's the difference between the SCRA and MLA benefit?
**Account-open date.** Opened the card **before** active duty → **SCRA** (6% cap + fee waiver, must be **requested**). Opened **during** active duty → **MLA** ($0 annual fee, **automatic**). They don't stack on one account.

#### Which cards qualify?
Any **personal** Amex card — including premium cards like Platinum, Gold, Delta SkyMiles Reserve, and Hilton Aspire — gets the MLA $0-fee adjustment for covered borrowers. **Business and corporate cards** do **not** get the fee benefit (though SCRA's rate cap can reach a pre-service business/corporate account).

#### Can I combine it with a promo code or sale?
There is no promo code or sale — this is a fee waiver, not a discount. Card welcome offers and points are unaffected and apply as normal.

#### What's actually the best move for a servicemember?
If you're active-duty or a military spouse and want an Amex, **apply while covered** so the MLA automatically makes the annual fee $0. If you already had the card before serving, **file an SCRA request** with your orders to cap your rate and recover fees.
